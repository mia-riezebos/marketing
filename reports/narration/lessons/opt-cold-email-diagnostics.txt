How to read a cold-email report without sending one

Christian’s cheat sheet is a diagnostic for why a cold-email campaign fails. Some of the “fixes” collide with inbox and privacy rules. This lesson teaches the reading, not the sending.

Takeaway: Bounces, replies, and interest diagnose different problems. A missing unsubscribe may improve a spreadsheet and still break the law.

Cold email means writing to people who did not ask to hear from you. Many places regulate it. Inboxes punish it. This course will not ask you to build a list or send a message.

Christian posted a short cheat sheet that is useful anyway, because it separates different kinds of failure. If you ever read a report, or decide the channel is not for you, you should know what the numbers are claiming.

Failure patterns

His mappings, in plain words:

- Zero replies and zero bounces: the mail was probably flagged before it really went out. You do not have a market signal yet. You have a delivery problem.
- Replies but no interest: people could read you, and they did not want the offer.
- Bounces over about 2 percent: the list is dirty (bad addresses, old data, purchased leftovers).
- Open rate: he treats it as a tracking pixel that can hurt whether you land in the inbox at all. Do not worship it.

Those four lines are the durable part of the sheet.

Benchmarks he names

He also lists results he treats as normal or good:

- One lead per 300 contacts is a great campaign; one per 500 is still solid
- Google-hosted prospects reply much more than Microsoft-hosted ones (he says about 6.5 times)
- Microsoft prospects want copy under 40 words
- Generic AI first lines reply at 0.27 percent
- Personalization at huge scale is fake
- A positive reply should be answered within five minutes
- A fourth follow-up that just says “Hi John???” is a thing people do (he lists it; that does not make it wise)

Treat those figures as his reported rules of thumb, not as a promise about your category.

The sharp and risky lines

He writes “unsubscribe link = swap it for reply ‘banana’.” The idea is that a visible unsubscribe link, in his view, hurts campaigns, so he would rather people reply with a code word.

That is a tactic. It is also a legal and trust problem in many places that require a working opt-out for commercial email, and in any place with privacy rules about how you obtained the address. Hiding the exit can also train people to mark you as spam, which is worse than a lost send.

Other listed moves (very fast follow-up, ultra-short copy for some inboxes, not using a tracking pixel) are operational. None of them make an unsolicited campaign automatically allowed.

If you do legitimate permission email later, the diagnostic still helps: bounces are a list problem, empty curiosity is an offer problem, and silence plus no bounces is delivery.

What to do

Under 2 minutes. Pair the three failure patterns with their causes.

Optional, about 8 minutes. Invent a small report, diagnose it, and write one sentence about the unsubscribe risk. Do not send anything.

You're done when

You can read the sheet as a diagnostic, and you know sending is outside this course.
