Your first month if you join a growth role

Honorato’s first-month roadmap is for someone joining a company that already has product-market fit. The saved archive is partial. What remains is research first, then foundations rather than a bag of tactics.

Takeaway: A new growth hire’s first job is understanding the company, not installing a stack of tricks.

Matias Honorato wrote a roadmap for succeeding in your first month as a growth marketing hire at a company that already has product-market fit. If you are building your own thing, the core course is enough and you can skip this.

The saved copy is partial. We can see his homework: get a broad picture of structure, business model, current growth strategy, and metrics; know competitors and the channels they use; and identify a first quick win. He divides that research into team, company analysis, and finding the first win. He starts the team section by asking who the founders are, what they do, and why they started.

Later calendar detail is cut off. We do not invent the missing weeks.

What survives at the end is the same principle as Balfour, which Honorato quotes: foundations are built on principles, not tactics. After a few weeks you should be comfortable with the product, the users, and the channels, and your work should maximize learning and the value you add to that team.

His other essay, on first-principles growth, already informed the process lesson in the core path.

What “first win” means here

A first win, in the saved research aim, is not a viral campaign. It is something small you can learn from because you now understand the product and the current channels. It might be fixing a confusing sentence on a page that already gets visits, or sitting with support notes long enough to hear the words customers already use.

If the archive had contained his later week-by-week calendar, we would teach it. It does not, so we stop.
