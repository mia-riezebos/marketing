# Go one circle in

About 7 minutes to read. Optional exercise about 8 minutes. These are estimates.

Seth Godin draws marketing as circles inside one another. Kunal Gandhi later called that drawing a golden blueprint and shared it. The idea is Seth’s. Kunal is the person who handed it to a later audience.

A tribe, here, means a group of people who already share a worldview. It is not a slogan for “our users.”

## The outer circle: shouting

Most people think marketing is this circle. The factory, the team, or the boss finishes something. Marketing’s job is to hype it, promote it, and yell about it.

Charities, candidates, insurance companies, and plenty of software teams live here. You can recognize the habit: the product is treated as fixed, and language is asked to do all the work.

Ads belong in a serious plan sometimes. They are not the whole map.

## The next circle in: a story a tribe can love

Here you tell a story that lines up with how some people already see the world. You admit the product is not for everyone. You work to make it easy for the right people to care.

This is uncomfortable if you want “everyone” as a market. It is also how people fall in love with something instead of merely noticing it.

## The next circle in: what surrounds the thing

The product is more than the object or the feature list. Support, packaging, onboarding, return policies, and atmosphere change what people think they bought.

Godin uses Zappos as a company that changed the surroundings of shoes (the buying and return experience). He uses IKEA as a brand that almost willfully does the opposite with assembly and delivery. An expensive restaurant sells more than the food. If you treat your product as a commodity, people will too.

## The innermost circle: the thing itself

When the product has communication built in, when it is remarkable enough to talk about, marketing feels easier. That is because you did marketing when you invented the thing. You are not paying extra to yell it into existence.

Godin’s advice when you feel stuck is simple. Go one circle in.

## A concrete example

A password manager that only runs ads about “bank-grade encryption” is on the outer circle. Encryption is real, and most tired people cannot feel it.

One circle in is a story for people who have reused the same password for a decade and are tired of being scared. One circle in from there is the surrounding experience: import that actually works, a recovery path that does not trap them, support that does not shame them. The innermost circle is a product that makes the safe choice the easy choice so people tell a friend without being asked.

If launches keep flopping, more ad copy is the outer-circle answer. Improving recovery or picking a sharper tribe is an inward move.

## Hypothetical Maal example

This example is invented for teaching.

An outer-circle line would be “AI meal planning for everyone.” One circle in is a story for people who already know ADHD makes 17:40 hard. One circle in from there is onboarding that decides dinner instead of assigning a planning project. The innermost circle is a product that removes the decision, which is the thing worth talking about.

## What to do

**Under 2 minutes.** Write which circle ate most of your time this month: outer, story, surroundings, or product.

**Optional, about 8 minutes.** Write one sentence per circle for your practice idea. If a circle is empty, write “empty.”

## You're done when

You can name the four circles and you have chosen an inward move you would try before more promotion.
