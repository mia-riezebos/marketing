# Fit the plan on one page

About 9 minutes to read. Optional exercise about 10 minutes. These are estimates.

Strategy documents go stale because they try to sound complete. A one-page brief stays useful because you can read it before you make the thing.

## GACC

Emily Kramer, writing for MKT1, calls her brief GACC: goals, audience, creative (the unique take), and channels (distribution). She evolved it while leading marketing at Asana and still uses it with startups she advises.

Write a GACC when you are planning a campaign, a blog post, an event, or anything longer than a tweet. Writing it first, she says, gives the work focus, sets context for anyone who reviews it, and forces a plan for results.

The rest of that newsletter is paywalled in this corpus. We do not invent her paid templates or the longer GACCS prompts. What is public is enough to use.

Kunal Gandhi later tells marketers to employ GACC (he writes it Goal, Audience, Creative, Channel) for any marketing task, then keep a board, work from a calendar, and measure. In a first-week plan he uses it on day two, next to messaging pillars such as what you build, why now, and who it is for.

Kramer authored the framework. Kunal is teaching people to use it.

## Two tests before you make the thing

Kramer offers two principles.

Everything you create should ladder up to a perception you want to build, or to a real goal. If it does not, deprioritize it for now.

Everything you create should pass an “add value” test for that specific audience. If it is only a duplicate of what already exists, ask whether it is worth making.

Those two tests will kill a surprising amount of busywork: the extra blog post with no reader in mind, the fifth social account, the event that exists because other companies throw events.

## How to write each letter

**Goal.** What change do you want, and how would you notice it? Jenny Romanchuk’s HubSpot explainer of SMART goals is a practical way to write this line: specific, measurable, achievable, relevant, and time-bound. “Get more awareness” is not a goal. “Learn whether people who already search for shared shopping lists understand our ‘end the milk fight’ sentence, by asking five people we already know” is closer, and it does not require a public campaign.

**Audience.** Not “everyone” and not “small businesses.” Who would care a lot, in the sense of the previous lessons? How would you recognize them?

**Creative / unique take.** This is not a color palette. It is the point of view that would not fit a competitor’s page. Often it is the enemy you named, or the customer phrase you copied.

**Channel.** Where will this specific piece live? One channel, not a spray. If you cannot name the place, you do not have a brief yet.

## A one-page strategy around the brief

Kunal also published a one-page crypto marketing template. In the post that announced it, he lists four blocks: brand promise, target audience, growth strategy, and marketing initiatives. He later posted a public Notion link. A partial extraction of that board recovered product promise, elevator pitch, and the start of a vision section. The rest of the board is not in this corpus, so we do not invent it.

You can build a beginner page from what we actually have:

- Product or brand promise (what change you are making)
- Elevator pitch (a short spoken version)
- Who it is for
- The GACC for the first thing you would create
- What you would measure
- What you are not doing yet

Blanks are allowed. A blank is more honest than a paragraph you copied from a competitor.

## A concrete example

A furniture repair shop wants a page for people moving out of apartments. Goal: see whether movers will mention them, measured by five conversations they already plan to have. Audience: movers and renters who have seven days. Unique take: same-week repair so the landlord does not keep the deposit. Channel: a one-page site plus a printed card, not four social apps.

## Hypothetical Maal example

This example is invented for teaching.

Goal: learn whether “dinner, decided” is understood as a decision-remover. Audience: people who already describe 17:40 as the hard part. Unique take: not another planner. Channel: a private draft page shown to two people the author already knows. No public launch in the exercise.

## What to do

**Under 2 minutes.** Write one line each for goal, audience, unique take, and channel, for a piece of work you will not publish this week.

**Optional, about 10 minutes.** Expand it into the one-page sketch above. Leave blanks.

## You're done when

You have a GACC, and you can use Kramer’s two tests to kill work that does not earn its place.
