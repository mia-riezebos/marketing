# Pick a vehicle for the story

About 9 minutes to read. Optional exercise about 10 minutes. These are estimates.

A vehicle, in Ryan’s language, is a channel you use to carry your story: X, YouTube, a newsletter, a podcast, a site you own. It is not the story itself. If you have no chapters to tell, a new account will not save you.

Ryan authored this distribution guide. The same person later wrote the copywriting guide that the next lesson uses.

## Chapters first

If you take only one thing from his guide, he writes, take this: building and distributing a brand is telling a series of chapters that fit a larger story. You are taking people on a journey. Each piece of organic content is one more step.

That is why founder-led content can work when it is honest. The person building the thing is living the next chapter.

Good storytelling, in his three buckets, makes people feel something, teaches them something, and makes them want the next chapter. Start from the end state you want people to buy into, then reverse-engineer the beliefs and chapters that would make that end state make sense. Your product roadmap, he says, is often your story roadmap. For a personal brand, the development itself can be the story, or the story of a path you already walked.

Voice is how you tell it. He advises sounding like yourself, and not borrowing a tone that is not yours.

## Rented, owned, and earned

Every vehicle, he writes, falls into one of three buckets.

**Rented.** Platforms you do not control, such as X, TikTok, Instagram, or YouTube. They are good for reach. They can change the rules overnight.

**Owned.** Audiences you can reach directly, such as a newsletter, a community you host, or your own site. They are better for trust and for asking someone to take a next step.

**Earned.** Other people’s audiences, such as a guest conversation or a collaboration. They are good for mindshare and for borrowing trust you have not built yet.

To build wide distribution over time, he says you want all three. To start, he says not to be everywhere. Begin with one or two platforms at most.

This matches the earlier one-channel lesson. A second vehicle can support the first (a newsletter that collects people you met on a platform). Opening five rented accounts on the same day is still spreading thin.

## How he thinks about common vehicles

These are his summaries, not a ranking you must obey.

X is strong for building a network and testing ideas quickly. You can try a chapter there and later expand it.

Short video can grow fast and suits people who are willing to be on camera.

Long-form YouTube is slow at the start and can compound.

LinkedIn is quieter than X in his telling, with an audience that often has buying power, and is worth considering for business-to-business work.

A newsletter is one of the few owned vehicles that can get attention right after you send it.

Podcasts help with depth and with borrowing another audience.

Choose with three questions:

- Where does my audience already spend time?
- Am I better at writing, talking, or being on camera?
- How much time can I actually give this?

If the honest answer to the third question is “twenty minutes a week,” do not pick a weekly YouTube essay plus a daily short-video plan. Pick the vehicle whose rhythm you can keep without turning it into a streak you resent.

## A concrete example

A furniture repair shop’s audience already searches when a landlord inspection is coming, and already talks to movers. Search and a simple owned page are vehicles. A daily TikTok of furniture content might be fun and still miss the person with seven days left on a lease.

Chapters might be: what a withheld deposit actually costs, a same-week repair photographed start to finish, and what movers wish renters knew. Those chapters can live on one page and in conversations long before a social account exists.

## Hypothetical Maal example

This example is invented for teaching.

Possible rented vehicles include communities where people already talk about ADHD and dinner. An owned vehicle might be an email for people who asked to hear more. An earned vehicle might be a conversation with someone who already writes about ADHD daily life. Circling “write where the dinner-time freeze is already being described” is a first-vehicle choice. Opening five apps the same week is not.

## What to do

**Under 2 minutes.** Name one rented, one owned, and one earned vehicle. Circle one to practice first.

**Optional, about 10 minutes.** Answer the three choice questions for that vehicle, and list two chapter ideas. Do not publish them.

## You're done when

You can explain the three buckets, and you have circled one vehicle without creating a new account for the exercise.
