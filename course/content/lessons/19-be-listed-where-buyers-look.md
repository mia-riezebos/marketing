# Be listed where buyers already look

About 11 minutes to read. Optional exercise about 12 minutes. These are estimates.

A listing is a public page about you on someone else’s site: a software directory, a launch board, a review platform, a community directory, or an integration marketplace. Some listings send curious buyers. Some only exist to create a link. Many do neither unless you already built something real.

Grant O published a long list of about 200 opportunities, sorted into roughly ten categories. His warning is the part to keep: these are places to investigate, not 200 guaranteed backlinks. Some are free. Others have paid tiers. Many require a qualifying business, product, or piece of writing.

Sol published another list of about 50 more specific places (communities, launch sites, review boards). The expanded destinations include well-known community hubs and discovery sites. Use Grant’s filter on any such list. Do not submit to everything that loads.

Natia Kurdadze shared a “high DA” list, meaning sites that scoring tools treat as authoritative. The list is mixed. G2, Capterra, Crunchbase, GitHub, and Trustpilot can be places buyers already look, if your product belongs there. Other entries are general profile, image, or publishing hosts. A high score is not the same thing as a choosing customer.

## How to approach a directory

Grant’s software-directory advice generalizes well.

Write one consistent product description you can reuse. Prepare screenshots. Pick categories that are true. Provide a working URL.

Do not treat every site as interchangeable. In his examples, AppSumo is a marketplace, some lists emphasize open source, and Product Hunt is built around launches. Prioritize platforms where the people you want are already comparing options.

If a site wants a launch date, a video, or a review from existing users, and you do not have those yet, skip it for now. A thin or misleading profile can follow you.

This course will not ask you to submit anything. The useful homework is the description and the fit list.

## Integration marketplaces are a different kind of listing

Taras describes three more specific routes for software that other tools can plug into.

On Albato, you need a public API with keys, you build the app in their integrator (triggers and actions from your endpoints), and you submit a description, logo, and test account for moderation.

On the Hermes agent marketplace, you need a remote MCP server (a way for an agent to call your product), a public GitHub repo with the expected plugin files, and a pull request that adds you to the catalog. He credits Aaron Delasy for finding this route. He also says reviewers look for you installing your own plugin and posting test results on the request. His listing, he writes, merged in less than a day. That timing is his report.

On Vercel’s skills listing, you write a SKILL.md that tells an agent how to use your API, put it in a public repo, and add it with their published command. You then get a page with an install command and a counter.

These are not “submit your URL to 50 sites” tactics. They require a real integration and a moderator or reviewer. If you do not have an API, they do not apply yet. That is a clean “does not apply,” not a failure.

Taras later wrote that Claude was sending him multiple customers a day and that the integrations were paying off. That is his claim. It is a reason to take marketplace pages seriously if you already have the plumbing. It is not a reason to fake an integration for a link.

## Risks worth naming

Some directories exist mainly to sell you a better placement later. Read the terms before you ever upload a logo.

Mass-submitting the same text to dozens of unrelated sites can look like spam to both people and search engines. The next optional lessons cover paid listicles and other sharp link tactics, including why some of those tactics can violate search-engine rules. This lesson stays on honest discovery pages.

Community lists (Discord, Reddit, Skool, and similar) only work if you participate as a person and follow house rules. Do not drop a product link into a stranger’s question as homework.

## A concrete example

A shared shopping-list app might belong on software directories where people compare household tools, and on a launch board if there is a real launch date. It probably does not belong on a Chrome-extension store unless it is an extension, or on an image host, or on an agent marketplace until there is an API an agent could use. The reusable paragraph would say who shares the kitchen, what fight it prevents, and that it is not a full budget planner.

## Hypothetical Maal example

This example is invented for teaching.

A fitting listing would say Maal decides dinner for people who already know ADHD makes 17:40 hard, and that it is not another meal planner. Review platforms for consumer apps might fit later. An agent marketplace would fit only if there were a real integration to submit.

## What to do

**Under 2 minutes.** Write a 60-to-80-word description, including who it is for and what it is not.

**Optional, about 12 minutes.** Pick five listing types from Grant’s kind of categories (software directories, launch platforms, review sites, communities, integration marketplaces, and similar). Mark each as a fit, with one reason, or as a skip. Do not submit.

## You're done when

You have a reusable description and a short fit list, and you can tell a buyer list from a score list.
