# Start here

About 6 minutes to read. Optional exercise about 2 minutes. These are estimates.

This course starts from zero on purpose. You do not need a marketing job title. You do not need an ad budget. You do not need to become an influencer or start an agency.

You do need something you want people to understand. That can be a product you are building, a service you already offer, or a practice idea you invent for the exercises. If you invent it, label it invented.

## How a lesson works

Each lesson teaches one idea.

1. Read the takeaway.
2. Read how the mechanism works.
3. Read the examples. Anything marked hypothetical is made up for teaching.
4. Do the under-2-minute action, or skip it.
5. Stop at “You’re done when.”

The optional exercise is extra practice, usually 5 to 15 minutes. It produces a finished artifact you can keep. It never asks you to publish, spend money, connect a paid account, or message strangers.

If a section is titled optional depth, you can skip it and still finish the lesson.

## Four labels you will see

**Method.** A process someone described. Useful when you can repeat the steps.

**Author’s claim.** A result, revenue figure, or “this always works” line. Treat it as that person’s report, not as a guarantee for you.

**Finding.** A described research result, still bound to that study’s setup.

**Our synthesis.** A recommendation we made after comparing sources. If other people shaped it, they are listed as “shaped by,” not as if they wrote our sentence.

When two people taught the same idea, both names stay. If someone only quoted another writer, the quoted writer keeps the authorship. Dan Koe authored the article Mogul shared. Guillaume Bardet authored the research method Roman shared. Ryan authored the copywriting guide Grant Miller shared.

## What “done” looks like in this course

Done means you produced the small artifact named in the lesson, or you honestly decided to stop after the reading. It does not mean you posted every day or filled a calendar.

A later app can remember which lessons you finished, which you bookmarked, and any notes you typed. That memory can live in your browser. It does not need an account, and it should not guilt you about a gap of days.

## A note about assistants

Later lessons cover working with AI agents. Even there, you keep judgment, promises, and anything that talks to a customer or spends money. An assistant can draft. You decide what is true.
