# A local-search plan, without the bankruptcy story

About 7 minutes to read. Optional exercise about 8 minutes. These are estimates.

Sarvesh Shrivastava posted a plan he would use, with Grok Bot, if he woke up bankrupt and needed to reach $100,000 a month. The income target and the personal framing are his claims. What transfers is the reminder that many buyers search with a place attached: “emergency plumber near me,” “same-week chair repair Utrecht.”

Local search uses the same hygiene you already learned (unique titles, pages search can read, titles that sound like the query). It adds:

- The service and the place in the title and the first paragraph
- A page per distinct job you actually do, rather than one vague “services” page
- Consistent name, address, and hours on any profile you later choose to claim
- Proof that you really work in that place (photos, a mapped area, a plain description of where you go)

You do not need his full stack or his income story. If your practice idea is not local, skip this module.

Do not create business profiles, scrape competitors, or run ads for the exercise.

## A concrete example

A chair-repair shop that only has a homepage titled “Welcome” is invisible for “same-week chair repair near Utrecht Science Park.” Three better pages might be: the main repair page, a page about pre-inspection work before a landlord visit, and a page that names the neighborhoods they actually travel to. Each title should include the job and, where it is true, the place. A photo of a repaired chair in a real hallway is more useful than a stock image of a workshop.

## What to do

**Under 2 minutes.** Write one service-plus-place phrase.

**Optional, about 8 minutes.** Sketch three local pages and their titles.

## You're done when

You can explain how a local page differs from a generic homepage.
